A bonsai is alive, and exporting life is a discipline of its own. Here is how a D'Uma tree travels from our garden to a collector abroad.

## Conditioning

Weeks before departure, the tree is prepared for the journey: root health verified, canopy secured, watering calibrated for the days it will spend without our hands. A tree that leaves stressed arrives stressed.

## Documentation

Every exported tree travels with official phytosanitary certification, plus whatever the destination country's import authority requires — including USDA compliance for the United States. Paperwork is unglamorous and non-negotiable.

## The crate

Each tree gets a custom-built crate: the pot immobilized, the canopy protected but ventilated, moisture managed. We have learned, shipment by shipment, exactly how much protection a living tree needs — and how much airflow it demands.

## The handover

On arrival, the collector receives not just a tree but its story: species, age, styling history, and care guidance for its new climate. Our responsibility does not end at the port.

Exporting bonsai is how Bali's living art reaches the world. We treat every shipment as an ambassador.
