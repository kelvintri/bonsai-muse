*Pemphis acidula* grows where almost nothing else dares — on sun-blasted limestone shores, in salt spray, clinging to rock. That struggle is written into every Santigi: bleached deadwood flowing like water beside a thin living vein, tiny leaves hardening against the wind.

## Why Santigi defines Indonesian bonsai

No other species tells the Indonesian story so completely. The deadwood (*jin* and *shari*) forms naturally in the wild; the artist's work is to reveal it, not invent it. A great Santigi looks as though the ocean carved it and a human merely framed the result.

## What to look for

- **Deadwood with direction** — the shari should flow with the trunk's movement, never against it.
- **Foliage in clouds** — small, dense pads that contrast the pale wood.
- **Nebari on stone** — the finest specimens grip rock, a reminder of their coastal origin.

## Caring for Santigi in the tropics

Full sun, sharp drainage, and restraint with water. Santigi forgives heat but not wet feet. In Bali's climate it grows vigorously — which means the artist's shears, not nature, set the pace.

When you stand before a great Santigi, you are looking at decades of shoreline weather, condensed into a pot. That is why collectors around the world seek Indonesian Santigi above all others.
