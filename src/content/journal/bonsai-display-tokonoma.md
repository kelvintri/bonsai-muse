In Japan, a bonsai is never displayed alone. It is composed — in a *tokonoma* alcove, with a scroll and an accent — as a landscape you can hold in your eyes.

## The three elements

- **The tree** — the mountain, the protagonist.
- **The scroll** — the sky, setting season and mood.
- **The accent** — the foreground: a grass, a stone, a small figure giving scale.

Remove any one and the composition collapses. Together they create depth no single object can.

## What this means for collectors

When you place a D'Uma tree in your home, you are not finding a spot for a plant. You are composing an alcove. Consider the wall behind it, the light that falls on it, the stand that lifts it to eye level. A masterpiece in the wrong corner is a whisper; in the right alcove, it is a voice.

## How we exhibit

At ROOTED and in our gallery, every tree is staged this way — scroll, stand, accent, light. It is slower to install and harder to photograph casually. It is also the only honest way to show what these trees are.
